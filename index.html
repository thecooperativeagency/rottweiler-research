01 — The problem

People started breeding them weird

Bigger heads. Heavier bone. Shorter muzzles. More bulk, less athlete. If you want the old dog, shop on purpose.

Search “Rottweiler puppy” long enough and the feed fills with dogs that barely resemble the breed’s working roots. Some are impressive. Many are oversized, overdone, or selected for a look that photographs tough and ages poorly.

The original Rottweiler was a German drover and butcher’s dog: compact, powerful, mobile, and steady. Strong enough to work. Balanced enough to live with. Serious enough to guard a home without needing a cartoon skull.

This guide is for buyers who want that older idea of the dog as a family companion and natural guardian, with real obedience training, and who are willing to ignore giant pet-mill aesthetics.

Compact power. Clean head. Calm nerves.

What “original” buyers usually want

  • Documented German / European bloodlines
  • Compact, athletic structure
  • Strong but proportional head
  • Confident family companion temperament
  • Natural guarding with an off-switch
  • Health testing you can verify

What to walk away from

  • “Giant” / mastiff-bone as the main pitch
  • Very short muzzles and extreme domed heads
  • Always-available litters and next-day shipping
  • No public hip, elbow, or JLPP results
  • Rare colors and long coat as selling points
  • Protection hype with no house manners story

Parent profile for a companion-guardian home

  1. Green: German or clear European pedigree · hips/elbows passed · JLPP clear · BH or breed test preferred · family-home placements.

05 — Process

How to buy without getting played

Slow is an advantage. Type is decided on adults, not eight-week fluff.

  1. Build a type board Save side profiles, fronts, and movement clips of adults you like.
  2. Touch the network USRC events and import-minded breeders beat Facebook marketplace culture.
  3. Study parents first Standing photos, movement video, pedigree, and health links for both sire and dam.
  4. Ask the same questions everywhere Compare answers. Vague breeders fail the test for free.
  5. Only then reserve — or buy a young adult Young adults often show temperament and structure more honestly.

Adult type checklist

  • Compact and athletic, not a meat slab
  • Muzzle proportional — not smashed
  • Moves freely on video
  • Not sold mainly on extreme size
  • Both parents shown standing and moving

Program checklist

  • Hips/elbows verified on OFA or German certs
  • JLPP results for both parents
  • Pedigree/import story is documentable
  • Family-home references available
  • Written contract and return clause

Questions worth asking every breeder

  1. Can I see OFA links or German HD/ED certificates for both parents?
  2. What are the JLPP results?
  3. Do you have standing photos and movement video of the sire and dam?
  4. How do you match puppies to companion / guardian homes?
  5. Can I speak with two recent family owners?
  6. What happens if the dog is the wrong fit after placement?

06 — Hard passes

Skip these and save years

Facebook “giant German” litters

Usually the bulk/type drift you are trying to escape, with thin paperwork.

“#1 USA / ship worldwide / deposit today”

Volume marketing over selection — even when the copy says “import bloodlines.”

No verifiable hips, elbows, or JLPP

Non-negotiable on this breed.

Rare colors or long coat as features

Wrong priorities for classic type.

Only pet papers, zero European/working story

Different selection culture than original German type.
